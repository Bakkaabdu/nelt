Design an immersive, interactive German-learning game called “Survival German — أنقذ نفسك بالألمانية” for my language-learning website.

The game must be accessible directly from the main website's top navigation bar (navbar). It should have a clearly visible, attractive navigation item labeled “Survival German” with a suitable game icon. Clicking it should open the game experience.

The initial version must support ONLY A1 and A2 German proficiency levels. Do not include B1 or higher levels anywhere in the playable content.

## 1. Core Concept

Survival German is an interactive life-simulation adventure in which students navigate realistic situations in Germany by communicating in German.

The player is a person who has just arrived in Germany and must learn to manage everyday life. Every mission presents a realistic scenario in which the player must understand German dialogue, select or construct appropriate responses, and complete practical tasks.

The experience should feel like an adventure game rather than a conventional language test.

The player should feel that they are actually living in Germany, interacting with people, solving everyday problems, and gradually becoming more independent.

The game should combine:
- Interactive storytelling.
- Realistic German conversations.
- Everyday vocabulary acquisition.
- Reading comprehension.
- Grammar practice through context.
- Decision-making and problem-solving.
- Progression, rewards, and achievements.
- A sense of adventure and discovery.

The entire experience should be enjoyable, visually engaging, educational, and accessible to beginners.

## 2. Placement in the Main Website

Integrate the game into the existing website's main navigation bar at the top of the homepage.

The navigation item should be easy to discover without overwhelming the website's other sections.

Give the game its own dedicated experience with a distinctive visual identity, while maintaining consistency with the website's overall design.

When students enter the game, present an engaging introduction explaining that they are about to begin their journey in Germany.

Allow them to choose between two difficulty levels:

- A1 — Beginner Survival
- A2 — Everyday Life in Germany

Clearly explain the difference between the two levels.

A1 should use simple vocabulary, short sentences, basic grammar, and straightforward situations.

A2 should introduce longer conversations, more detailed situations, additional vocabulary, past-tense expressions, and slightly more complex sentence structures.

Students should be able to see their progress in each level.

## 3. The Game's Story

Create an engaging narrative that begins when the player arrives in Germany for the first time.

The player has limited knowledge of German and must gradually learn how to navigate everyday situations.

Start at Berlin Brandenburg Airport.

The player must figure out how to communicate with airport staff, find transportation, buy a train ticket, reach their destination, purchase groceries, order food, visit a doctor, and eventually search for accommodation and attend a job interview.

Each completed mission should unlock new parts of the player's journey.

Use short narrative scenes between missions to establish continuity. The player should feel that the missions are connected parts of one larger adventure rather than unrelated exercises.

Include occasional unexpected events, misunderstandings, practical problems, and small decisions that make the experience more engaging.

Keep the scenarios realistic and appropriate for language learners.

## 4. Mission Structure

Create the following seven main missions.

IMPORTANT: Every mission must be LONG, detailed, rich in content, and enjoyable to play.

Do not create short demonstrations consisting of two or three questions.

Each mission should feel like a substantial interactive chapter, containing multiple scenes, several conversations, numerous vocabulary items, varied exercises, realistic challenges, and a satisfying conclusion.

Each mission should take approximately 10–20 minutes to complete, depending on the student's pace.

Include approximately 15–25 meaningful interactive challenges per mission, distributed naturally throughout the story. Vary the number when necessary to make the narrative feel natural.

Every challenge should contribute to the story or reinforce something the student needs to learn.

### MISSION 1: Arriving at the Airport

Location: Berlin Brandenburg Airport.

The player has just arrived in Germany and needs to find their way around.

Include detailed interactive scenes involving:

- Understanding airport signs.
- Greeting airport employees.
- Asking where the exit is.
- Asking where the train station is.
- Understanding basic announcements.
- Asking for directions.
- Understanding numbers and times.
- Asking someone to repeat a sentence.
- Explaining that they do not understand German well.
- Asking someone to speak more slowly.
- Understanding simple questions about their destination.
- Identifying important places inside the airport.
- Responding appropriately to common questions.
- Handling a misunderstanding with an airport employee.
- Finding the correct route to the train station.

Example conversation:

Airport employee:
“Guten Tag! Kann ich Ihnen helfen?”

The player must choose an appropriate response.

Possible answers:
1. “Ja, bitte. Wo ist der Bahnhof?”
2. “Ich bin dreiundzwanzig Jahre alt.”
3. “Ich esse gern Pizza.”

If the player selects the correct response, the conversation continues naturally.

If the player makes a mistake, explain why the answer is incorrect and provide a useful hint.

Include several conversations that become gradually more challenging as the mission progresses.

End the mission when the player successfully finds the train station and understands how to continue the journey.

Include practical conversations between the player and ticket-office staff, railway employees, and other passengers.

Use realistic German expressions and explain their meanings in Arabic.

Make the mission progressively more difficult while remaining appropriate for the selected level.

End the mission when the player successfully boards the correct train.

End the mission when the player completes the shopping trip successfully.

### MISSION 5: Visiting a Doctor

## 6. Two Difficulty Levels: A1 and A2 Only

The game must support exactly two playable proficiency levels in its initial version.

### A1 — Beginner Survival

Use:
- Short, simple sentences.
- Common everyday vocabulary.
- Basic greetings and introductions.
- Numbers, prices, times, and dates.
- Basic questions and answers.
- Present-tense verbs.
- Simple word order.
- Basic articles and pronouns.
- Essential modal expressions where appropriate.
- Very short conversations that gradually become more complex.
- Clear explanations in Arabic.

The goal is to help a beginner survive common everyday interactions with basic German.

### A2 — Everyday Life in Germany

Use:
- Longer sentences and dialogues.
- Broader everyday vocabulary.
- More detailed descriptions.
- Common past-tense expressions.
- Modal verbs and everyday requests.
- More complex but accessible questions.
- Basic subordinate clauses where appropriate.
- Conversations requiring more independent responses.
- Practical reading comprehension.
- Situations involving clarification, explanations, and follow-up questions.

The goal is to help students handle common everyday situations with greater independence.

IMPORTANT:
Do not introduce B1-level content into either mode. The A2 mode should be more challenging than A1 without becoming unnecessarily advanced.

## 7. Arabic Support

The website is intended for learners who may speak Arabic as their first language.

Provide Arabic translations and explanations wherever they genuinely help the student understand the German.

For important German expressions, display:

- The original German expression.
- Its Arabic translation.
- A brief explanation of when it is used.
- A simple example of its use in another context.

For difficult vocabulary, provide optional Arabic hints.

However, do not translate every sentence automatically before the student has attempted to understand it.

Allow the student to reveal translations and hints when needed.

The purpose is to support learning while gradually encouraging direct comprehension in German.

Keep the Arabic text readable, properly aligned, and visually distinct from German text.

## 8. Three Lives and Mistake Handling

Give the player three hearts at the beginning of each mission.

When the player answers incorrectly:
- Explain the mistake clearly.
- Show a useful hint.
- Allow another attempt when appropriate.
- Reduce the number of hearts according to the game's rules.
- Do not immediately terminate the entire adventure after a single mistake.

When all hearts are lost, provide a clear opportunity to retry the mission.

Avoid making the experience frustrating or punishing.

The purpose of the game is learning, not penalizing beginners.

Correct answers should receive points.

Give additional rewards for:
- Completing a mission without losing a heart.
- Answering without revealing a hint.
- Correctly completing a final challenge.
- Revisiting previous missions and improving the result.

Use three stars to evaluate mission performance.

Explain how the student earned their stars.

## 9. Progression and Rewards

Build a clear progression system.

Students should be able to see:
- Completed missions.
- Locked and unlocked missions.
- Their current level.
- Their total points.
- Their number of remaining hearts during a mission.
- Their best result for each mission.
- Their earned stars.
- Their achievements.

Completing a mission should unlock the next mission.

Allow students to replay completed missions.

Reward improvement rather than merely rewarding students who already know the material.

Include meaningful achievement names such as:
- First Steps in Germany.
- Train Ticket Expert.
- Smart Shopper.
- Restaurant Regular.
- Everyday Communicator.
- Interview Ready.
- Finding a Home.

Keep achievements connected to the story.

## 11. Interactive Dialogue Design

Conversations must be central to the game.

When an NPC speaks, show the German sentence clearly.

Allow the student to choose from several possible responses or construct a response using provided words when appropriate.

After the student responds:
- Continue the dialogue naturally.
- Show the result of the choice.
- Explain the correct expression.
- Update the player's progress.
- Present the next part of the scenario.

Where suitable, provide more than one acceptable response.

Do not incorrectly mark a grammatically correct and contextually appropriate alternative as wrong simply because it differs from one preferred answer.

Ensure that the dialogue feels coherent across multiple exchanges.

Use believable everyday German rather than unnatural sentences written solely to test grammar.

## 12. Educational Quality

The game must teach usable German.

Prioritize:
- Practical communication.
- Vocabulary in context.
- Understanding everyday questions.
- Building complete sentences.
- Recognizing common expressions.
- Applying grammar naturally.
- Reading signs, advertisements, menus, and simple messages.
- Learning how to ask for clarification.
- Recovering from communication misunderstandings.

Reinforce previously introduced vocabulary in later challenges.

Provide explanations that are short enough to understand but detailed enough to teach something useful.

Avoid introducing too many unfamiliar words at once.

All German dialogue, vocabulary, grammar, and answer explanations must be appropriate for the selected level.

Check that each correct answer is genuinely correct and that distractors are clearly distinguishable without relying on arbitrary tricks.
